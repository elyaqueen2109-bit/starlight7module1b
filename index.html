📝 Grammar

Stative verbs

State verbs describe states, feelings, thoughts, or possessions. They are NOT usually used in Continuous forms.

  • Feelings: like, love, hate, prefer, want, need
  • Thoughts: know, understand, believe, think, remember, forget, mean
  • Senses: see, hear, smell, taste, feel
  • Possession: have, own, belong, contain
I want to be a UFO hunter. (NOT: I am wanting)

Some verbs can be both stative and dynamic — with a difference in meaning:

  • think — I think he's clever. (believe) / I am thinking of joining a UFO club. (considering)
  • have — He has a telescope. (possess) / He is having a look at the sky. (action)
  • see — I see a strange object. (perceive) / I am seeing Paula later today. (meet)
  • taste — The food tastes delicious. (state) / The cook is tasting the food. (action)
  • smell — It smells strange. (state) / She is smelling the flowers. (action)
  • look — He looks good. (appear) / What are you looking at? (action)

Present Simple vs Present Continuous

Present Simple — permanent states, facts, habits, timetables.

  • Nick comes from the USA.
  • He goes to a UFO hotspot every weekend.
  • The train leaves at 7 pm.

Time expressions: every day, on Mondays, often, usually, always, sometimes.


Present Continuous — actions happening now/around the time of speaking, future arrangements, temporary situations.

  • Nick is watching the sky right now.
  • He is meeting his UFO club tonight.
  • Jane is working as a waitress for the summer.

Time expressions: now, at the moment, at present, tonight, this week.

UFO Hunter!

A strange shape is moving across the sky. Is it a bird? Is it an aeroplane? Or is it a UFO? Nick Porter is fascinated by the possibility of life on other planets, so in his free time he tries to find out what these objects are. We asked him some questions about his unusual hobby.

So, Nick, why are you interested in UFOs?
Well, thousands of people see strange objects in the sky all over the world. Most of these are planets, meteors, or military planes — but what about the rest? I'm a very curious person, so I want to find an explanation!

And what exactly does your hobby involve?
Well, I started a UFO club and we mostly investigate sightings. We interview witnesses and analyse videos and photos. Sometimes we go out to a UFO hotspot, too. In fact, we're going out tonight. We usually go high up on a rooftop or a hillside. If we see something strange, we record as much information as we can. We use camcorders, cameras, telescopes, and other devices. Then we analyse the information on our laptops.

Maybe some of our readers are thinking about taking up UFO hunting now! What advice do you have for them?
Well, anyone can become a UFO hunter. You just need to be enthusiastic and patient. You also need to join a local UFO club. This way, you find out where the UFO hotspots are, and you have witnesses if you spot something.

So, what are you waiting for? UFO hunting is a lot of fun, and you never know — you might just turn science fiction into science fact!
